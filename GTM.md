# Go-To-Market Strategy

## Exact target user
Engineering manager or CTO at a 5-50 person seed-to-Series A startup. Has purchasing authority for software tools. Gets a monthly credit card reconciliation email. Has a vague sense AI tool costs are growing but no framework for evaluating them. Job titles: "Head of Engineering," "VP Engineering," "CTO." Secondary: indie hackers paying for 3-4 AI tools personally.

## What they Google before wanting this tool
- "cursor vs github copilot 2025"
- "is claude pro worth it for developers"
- "chatgpt team plan vs individual"
- Scrolling HN "Ask HN: what AI tools does your team actually use"

## Where they hang out
- **Hacker News** — Ask HN threads about AI tools. Exactly our ICP.
- **r/ExperiencedDevs**, **r/SideProject**
- **Indie Hackers** — forum and Slack
- **Lenny's Newsletter** Slack community
- **X/Twitter** — following @levelsio, @patio11, @swyx

## First 100 users in 30 days, $0 budget

**Week 1:** Show HN — "Show HN: StackAudit — free audit of your AI tool spend." HN loves tool audits and "I built this because I was annoyed" stories. Target: 30-50 users.

**Week 2:** Direct X outreach. Search "cursor pricing," "is claude pro worth it." People tweeting about AI tool decisions are actively evaluating. DM 20 with a specific message. Target: 10-15 users.

**Week 3:** Indie Hackers post — "I analyzed 50 startup AI stacks — here's where people overspend most." Share real anonymized data. Posts with data consistently drive 50-200 visitors.

**Week 4:** X thread with aggregate findings. Target: 20-30 users.

## Unfair distribution channel
The Credex customer network. Every existing Credex customer is a warm lead. An email to the customer list ("Free: audit your AI spend before your next renewal") drives 50+ audits from people already paying for AI infrastructure. No other candidate has access to this.

## Week-1 traction if this works
- 50+ audits on Show HN launch day
- 15+ email captures (30% conversion)
- 3+ Credex consultation bookings
- 5+ organic shares on X from users who found surprising savings
